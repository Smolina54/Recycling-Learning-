Waste Stream Training · 5 Streams · 5-Phase Sort

Know your bin.
Sort it right.

A five-minute walkthrough of the waste streams used across your building, followed by a short check to confirm what stuck. One wrong item can contaminate an entire bin — this is how we avoid that.

Stream 01 — Landfill

General Waste

Anything that can't be recycled, composted, or handled as e-waste goes here. This is the last resort stream — the goal across every floor is to keep it as small as possible.

✓ Goes in

  • Food-soiled wrappers and packaging
  • Chip packets and soft plastic (unless your floor has a soft-plastics point)
  • Broken ceramics, glass, or mirrors
  • Used tissues and disposable non-recyclable cups
  • Disposable PPE (masks, gloves)

✕ Never goes in

  • Clean bottles, cans, paper, or cardboard
  • Food scraps or liquids
  • Batteries or electronics
  • Anything with a recycling or organics symbol
💡General Waste is the most expensive stream to send to landfill. If a desk bin is filling up fast with General Waste, it's usually a sign recyclables are ending up in the wrong place.
Stream 02 — Commingled

Mixed Recycling

Rigid containers only — bottles, cans, and jars. The single rule that matters most here: clean and dry. Food residue is the #1 reason a whole bin of recycling gets rejected and sent to landfill instead.

✓ Goes in

  • Empty plastic bottles and containers
  • Aluminium and steel cans
  • Glass bottles and jars
  • Rinsed takeaway containers

✕ Never goes in

  • Soft plastics — bags, cling wrap, bubble wrap
  • Paper or cardboard (own stream)
  • Coffee cups (plastic-lined, not recyclable here)
  • Anything still holding food or liquid
💡A single unrinsed container can contaminate the containers around it. "Empty and dry" beats "spotless" — a quick shake-out is enough.
Stream 03 — Fibre

Paper & Cardboard

Dry, clean paper fibre — office paper, envelopes, and flattened boxes. Grease and food contact are the two things that disqualify a piece of paper or cardboard from this stream.

✓ Goes in

  • Office paper, envelopes, printouts
  • Newspapers and magazines
  • Cardboard boxes — flattened
  • Cardboard sleeves and folders

✕ Never goes in

  • Greasy or food-stained cardboard (e.g. pizza box → General Waste)
  • Used tissues or paper towel
  • Glossy, laminated, or plastic-coated paper
  • Paper cups
💡Flatten boxes before binning them. A folded box takes a fraction of the space and means the bin gets collected less often.
Stream 04 — Compost

Organics

Food scraps and certified compostable packaging, kept separate so they can actually break down instead of sitting sealed in landfill.

✓ Goes in

  • Fruit and vegetable scraps
  • Coffee grounds and tea bags
  • Leftover food (no liquids)
  • Certified compostable packaging only

✕ Never goes in

  • Liquids — tip these down a sink first
  • Standard plastic packaging
  • Plastic labelled "biodegradable" but not certified compostable
  • Cutlery, straws, or plastic-lined cups
💡"Biodegradable" and "compostable" are not the same claim — only packaging with a certified compostable logo belongs in this stream.
Stream 05 — Hazardous / Electronic

E-Waste

Batteries and electronics contain materials that are dangerous in landfill and valuable when recovered properly. These never go in a general bin — they go to a dedicated collection point.

✓ Goes in

  • Batteries — all types
  • Cables, chargers, adaptors
  • Phones, laptops, small electronics
  • Printer and toner cartridges

✕ Never goes in

  • Any general waste or recycling bin
  • Bulk furniture or large appliances (arrange separate pickup)
💡A single battery in the wrong bin can start a fire in a waste truck. If you're not sure where the e-waste point is on your floor, ask your Waste Champion.
